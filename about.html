How I Got Here

My collegiate career began where most people’s does: orientation. During orientation, I vividly remember deciding which sessions I should attend during the “free-time” between lunch and scheduling classes. My best friend and I decided to go to a session about Hall Council and Student Government because nothing else really sounded exciting. It was in this session that I knew I had found something I would do throughout my undergraduate career.

Following move-in, I ran for and served on my hall’s council. Through this experience, I connected with various mentors that shaped my experiences and opportunities immensely. During that first year, I was initiated into the National Residence Hall Honorary and immediately elected into the National Communications Coordinator position (which I served in until I graduated). From those experiences, I had found a strong passion for student affairs and began looking at it as a possible career choice.

During my Junior year, I was a Resident Assistant in my building. The year after, I was the Undergraduate Assistant Hall Director for the same building. These two roles solidified that I wanted to do this work after graduation, and following conversations with a few mentor figures; I applied and was accepted into Kansas State University’s Counseling and Student Development program. Shortly after, I applied, interviewed, and was offered the graduate assistantship of Assistant Coordinator for Departmental Initiatives in the Honors’ House on K-State’s campus.

Moving across the country for graduate school was certainly a choice, one that my family might not have fully understood. However, after spending three days on-campus for interviews; I knew that K-State was something special and that I needed to be there.

So here we are, four years and a degree and a half later. As I finish up coursework in my PhD; I often find myself reflecting on the path that led me here. What opportunities I’ve had, who I’ve had the ability to work with, and the impact I’ve been able to have on students are the most common thoughts that come to mind. A quote that I’ve included in most personal statements I’ve written is from Bishop T.D. Jakes that I feel summarizes the journey that brought me here:

"If you can't figure out your purpose, figure out your passion. For your passion will lead you right into your purpose."

— Bishop T.D. Jakes

What I Value

Mentorship

Mentorship is one of those things that everyone sort of values, but to me; the impact mentorship has on individuals is foundational to the work I do. Every conversation, interaction, and goal is derived from a place of "how does this help me become a better mentor or develop them into a stronger mentor for someone else?"

Community Building

The phrase "it takes a village" resonates strongly with me and the work I do. Students need to have a community to connect with and lean on when life happens. As a professional in the field, it is my job to not only encourage connection, but actively foster it through my actions. Whether that is planning and facilitating large scale connection events (like Activities Carnival) or meeting 1:1 with students to catch up and give them a safe space to share; everything I do is built upon community.

Sense of Belonging

Hand-in-hand with Community Building, students rely on a strong sense of belonging to succeed in life and academics. There is plenty of research highlighting the importance of fostering a sense of belonging for students; mainly the positive relationship between it and success (both academically and personally). As a professional, ensuring student success relies on developing a sense of belonging for all students.
